# Freeman Eclectic — Brand Summary

Reference for anyone writing copy, choosing photography, or making product and site decisions for
Freeman Eclectic. Source of record is `Freeman_Eclectic_Business_Philosophy.docx`; everything marked
**Proposed** below came out of brainstorming and has not been adopted.

## In One Sentence

> We create distinctive, heirloom-quality products that bring people together, reflect the character
> of Minnesota and America, and are worthy of being used, shared, and passed down.

## What Freeman Eclectic Is

A one-person Minnesota craft business — a retirement business, which shapes every decision about
time, pricing, and scope. It makes games, gifts, and keepsakes designed to get friends and families
sitting down together, away from computer screens.

The work is produced with CNC and laser equipment and finished by hand. "Crafted" is a deliberate
word: modern tools increase precision and possibility, but they do not remove the maker's
responsibility for design, material choice, fit, finish, and quality.

## What It Makes

- **Family games and shared activities** — cribbage boards, Farkle, Wahoo, Mancala, Coin Drop,
  License Plate Hunt, and other hardwood games built to be taught to a child or grandchild and kept.
- **Minnesota and outdoor pieces** — cabins, lakes, the North Shore, Boundary Waters, wildlife,
  lighthouses, local history and communities.
- **Coffee and conversation items** — engraved slate coasters and drinkware, lighthearted humor.
- **Heritage pieces** — respectful patriotic work and classic Americana.
- **Custom and commissioned work** — weddings, anniversaries, retirements, memorials, business
  awards, club and organization recognition.

Materials chosen to age well: cherry, walnut, maple, slate, glass, stainless steel.

## Where It Sells

Craft shows, consignment retailers, custom orders, and wholesale. A product has to fit the channel
it is offered in — something that works in a booth may not work on a retailer's shelf, and the
reverse.

## Purpose

Freeman Eclectic exists to make meaningful products that encourage friends and families to spend
time together away from screens — objects that create conversation, laughter, tradition, and lasting
memories. The point is not to fill a booth or add another item to a shelf. It is to make things that
earn a place in people's homes and lives.

## Core Principles

1. **Quality before shortcuts.** Finished quality is the first production standard. Faster machining
   or simplified production is valuable only when it does not meaningfully reduce finished quality.
2. **Distinctive, not disposable.** Products should look recognizably Freeman Eclectic — not clip art
   on cheap material, not a fad copied for a quick sale. Designs need enough character and permanence
   to still appeal after the trend cycle passes.
3. **Made to connect people.** Favor products that invite people to sit down together, teach someone,
   continue a tradition, give a thoughtful gift, or remember a meaningful place or event.
4. **Rooted in place and shared values.** Minnesota landscapes, wildlife, communities, history, cabin
   life, the North Shore, and respectful patriotic themes. Celebrate belonging without becoming
   politically charged, divisive, offensive, or demeaning.
5. **Crafted with intention.** Judgment, design, modern equipment, skilled production, and hands-on
   finishing — all of it, together.

## The Product Standard

Before a product joins the regular line, it should pass every one of these:

- Is it useful, meaningful, enjoyable, or memorable to the customer?
- Does it support family connection, Minnesota or patriotic identity, a lasting tradition, or a
  genuine customer need?
- Does the design feel original and visually strong rather than generic or fad-driven?
- Are the materials, machining, engraving, assembly, and finish consistent with the price and the
  quality promise?
- Can it be produced reliably and profitably without unreasonable time pressure?
- Does it fit the sales channel where it will be offered?
- Would we be proud to have this product represent Freeman Eclectic several years from now?

## How Business Decisions Get Made

Every product has to serve both the customer and the health of the business. Because this is a
one-person business, time saved is money — but only when efficiency protects quality and makes the
work more sustainable.

- Products are judged on material cost, active production time, total labor, selling price, retailer
  commission, margin, and return on production time.
- Decisions use actual sales results across craft shows, consignment, custom orders, and wholesale —
  not enthusiasm.
- Strong sellers get production and display priority. Slow sellers are redesigned, limited to the one
  channel where they perform, moved to the inactive list, or discontinued.
- Skilled time is never undervalued. Custom and large orders must cover design, testing, production,
  finishing, risk, and the opportunity cost of other work.
- Investment in equipment, materials, and process is justified by quality, consistency, safety,
  capacity, or meaningful profitability.

## Customer and Community Commitments

- **Honest value.** A fair price for a well-made product. We do not compete by pretending that
  skilled work, good materials, and production time have no value.
- **Dependable relationships.** Clear communication, realistic commitments, protected approved
  designs, and long-term relationships with retailers, organizations, and buyers rather than
  one-time transactions.
- **Generosity with purpose.** Donations and community projects — especially faith, youth, veterans,
  first responders, families, and local communities — kept intentional, documented, and sustainable.
- **Respect for the customer.** Products and marketing stay welcoming, positive, accurate, and
  family-friendly, so customers are comfortable buying for their own homes or giving to people they
  care about.

## What Freeman Eclectic Will Not Become

- A business that chases every fad or copies whatever is selling in the next booth.
- A producer of cheap, disposable items whose only advantage is a low price.
- A brand built on political conflict, offensive humor, or division.
- A shop that accepts unprofitable work simply to stay busy.
- A product line so broad that quality, consistency, or the joy of making is lost.

## Long-Term Direction

Grow by becoming more recognizable and more trusted — not merely larger. The goal is a focused
collection of sought-after products known for distinctive design, dependable quality, and the
experiences they create. Growth should strengthen the best parts of the business: heirloom-quality
games, meaningful Minnesota and patriotic work, carefully chosen gifts, rewarding custom projects,
and lasting relationships with customers and retail partners.

> The final measure is simple: make work that is worth our time, worthy of our name, and worth
> keeping.

## Voice and Tone

Warm, plain, and confident. Talk about materials, craft, and the occasions the work is made for.
Humor is lighthearted, clever, and welcoming — never insulting or mean-spirited. Do not oversell,
and do not apologize for the price.

Ideas from earlier brainstorming, kept here so they are not lost, but not policy:

- **Taglines:** "Crafted to Bring People Together," "Building Memories Beyond the Screen," "Creating
  Memories, One Project at a Time," "Handcrafted for Conversation," "Made to Gather Around."
